The chemistry-annotation release: movable charge marks, electron-pushing mechanism arrows, and a full Copy As menu.

- **Charge marks that behave like chemistry:** anions and cations are placed as movable ⊕/⊖ symbols that associate with the nearest atom that can legally carry them — drop a minus next to a drawn OH and it becomes the alkoxide, drag the mark away and the proton comes back. No stuck valence errors. Radical dots, radical ions (•+/•−), plain +/− marks, and lone pairs live behind one toolbar dropdown, and the octet arithmetic honors ionic charges on metals.
- **Electron-pushing arrows:** curved mechanism arrows (full-headed and radical fishhook) that anchor to atoms or charge marks and stay attached as the structure moves. One or two Bezier handles — depending on length — let the arrow snake around whatever is in the way. Esc cancels a half-drawn arrow.
- **Copy As:** SMILES, InChI, InChI Key, CDXML, MOL (V2000 and V3000, radicals included), SVG, and PNG — from the Edit menu or a right-click, with keyboard shortcuts. SVG pastes into Illustrator as editable vector art with labels and halos intact; PNG pastes at the drawn size at full 4× resolution. A selection carries its charge marks and mechanism arrows along, and right-clicking a multi-selection keeps the whole selection for the copy.
- **Atom relabeling with the text tool:** click any atom with the text tool, type an element symbol, press Enter — the atom takes its new identity.
- **Clipboard fixes:** structures copied between two running ChemDraft instances no longer paste as garbled characters, and pasted CJK text survives the trip.

Automatic updates are delivered via Sparkle (File ▸ Check for Updates…).
